LODESTAR — DEMO NARRATION
Nine scenes, about 4:00 total. One paragraph per scene; make_voiceover.py renders
each to its own MP3 and prints its duration, so video can be cut to the audio.

Scenes are separated by a line containing only ---


=== SCENE 1 — The problem (0:00–0:20)
On screen: title card, then a wall of course thumbnails.

Search any platform for machine learning and you get thousands of courses. Every
one is relevant. None of them tell you what to do first. Recommendation answers
"what is relevant to me", and it answers with similarity. Learners need
something else: a sequence. What must come first, and what am I allowed to start
only after. Lodestar answers the second question.

---

=== SCENE 2 — Intake (0:20–0:50)
On screen: the intake chat; the profile card filling in field by field.

It starts in plain English. "Second year CS student, I know Python and git, I
want to be an ML engineer, six hours a week, free resources only." Watch the
panel on the right. Goal, hours, experience, prior knowledge, cost — each
appears as it is extracted, and nothing appears that was not actually said. An
unstated field stays empty and the assistant asks for it, because a fabricated
field is worse than a missing one.

---

=== SCENE 3 — Diagnostic (0:50–1:20)
On screen: the diagnostic; deliberately answer one question wrong.

Self-report is the weakest signal in education technology, so we measure
instead. This question is not random: it targets the skill with the highest
uncertainty times the highest downstream impact. Measuring something that
unblocks fifty other skills is worth more than measuring a leaf. Watch the
confidence meter. And note that "I don't know" is a real button, because
guessing pollutes the measurement.

---

=== SCENE 4 — The path and the Why chip (1:20–2:20)
On screen: the skill graph, then the weekly timeline, then a Why chip expanded.

This is a dependency graph, not a ranked list. A hundred and fifty two skills,
two hundred and fifty five prerequisite edges, and the highlighted route is a
topological ordering of the gap between where this learner actually is and where
they want to be, packed into the six hours a week they actually have. Every step
carries a reason, and the reason is not a paragraph a model invented. Open the
Why chip. The dependency chain to the goal, computed from the graph. The
measured level, with the question ids behind it. The resource, how many
alternatives it beat, and why. The week, and what it unlocks. The model is shown
only this object and asked for two sentences, so it cannot state a reason the
data does not support. Pull the API key and the same reason still appears, from
a template.

---

=== SCENE 5 — Adaptation (2:20–2:50)
On screen: mark an item "too easy", then fail a milestone. Zoom the diff banner.

Feedback has to do something visible. Marking this step as already known raises
the mastery, drops the skill, and pulls everything after it forward. The banner
shows exactly that: one item removed, forty-three moved, finish a week earlier.
Now fail a checkpoint, and remediation returns. Each event writes a new version
rather than editing the old one, which is what makes the difference computable
at all.

---

=== SCENE 6 — What if (2:50–3:10)
On screen: drag the hours slider from six to eighteen.

A path without a calendar is a wish list. This slider asks a question rather
than making a decision: it re-runs the whole planner at a hypothetical capacity
and shows the new finish week live. Six hours a week finishes in week
forty-seven. Eighteen finishes in week sixteen. Nothing is saved until the
learner commits.

---

=== SCENE 7 — Dashboard (3:10–3:30)
On screen: the dashboard.

Progress, hours done against hours remaining, mastery by track averaged over
only the skills this goal requires, and the next three actions. The activity
feed is a direct read of the event log. Every state change is appended there,
which is why nothing about the plan can change silently.

---

=== SCENE 8 — Architecture and evaluation (3:30–3:50)
On screen: the layer diagram, then the evaluation table.

Deterministic algorithms do the reasoning; the model does the language. Twenty
synthetic personas with hand-written gold paths give us: zero prerequisite-order
violations, a hundred percent goal coverage, zero redundancy, path length within
three percent of gold, and p95 generation under four milliseconds. Four hundred
and twenty six resources, every URL checked with a real HTTP request. Knowing
where not to use a model is the point. A topological sort is not a machine
learning problem.

---

=== SCENE 9 — Close (3:50–4:00)
On screen: the live URL and the repository.

Lodestar. Learning is a dependency graph, not a search result. Live at the link
on screen, source on GitHub, and it runs end to end with no API key at all.
